# Draft selection

You pick from the options that exist. You don't rewrite them, and you never ask for a fresh set because none is perfect.

## Step 1: knockouts
Drop any draft that:
- breaks the brief (wrong platform, tone, deliverable, or a stated constraint)
- states a claim that isn't in the findings or the user's words (see `fact-grounding`)
- exceeds the platform limit (see `platform-specs`)

If every draft is knocked out, pick the one with the smallest fault and say what's wrong with it.

## Step 2: score what's left (1-3 each)
1. **Hook:** does the first line, the part seen before "more", make a specific promise or create real tension? A generic opener ("We're excited to announce") scores 1.
2. **Audience fit:** would the audience in the brief care, and does it sound like them?
3. **Call to action:** one clear next step that suits the platform.
4. **Distinct value:** says something the other options don't.

If the brief includes a video, add a fifth check: can the core idea be spoken in 50 words or fewer?

## Step 3: break ties
Prefer the draft that is more specific, then the one that is shorter.

## Say it
Tell the user in one or two sentences which draft you chose and why, and that they can pick another. Example: "Going with option 2: its opening line names the benefit and the call to action is a single step. Say the word if you'd prefer another."

Then have the copywriter record the pick.
